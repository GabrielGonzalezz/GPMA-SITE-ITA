Gabriel Felipe Mendes de Brito e González Pozzoni holds a degree in Science, Technology and Innovation from Ilum School of Science of the Brazilian Center for Research in Energy and Materials (CNPEM), Campinas. He is currently a special student in the M.Sc. programme in Aeronautical and Mechanical Engineering at ITA, focusing on adaptive structures, advanced manufacturing and experimental methods in engineering.

During a summer internship at the Brazilian Synchrotron Light Laboratory (LNLS/Sirius), on the EMA beamline (Extreme Conditions Methods of Analysis) under Dr Ricardo Donizeth dos Reis, he contributed to scientific instrumentation for X-ray diffraction under extreme pressure (up to 100 GPa) and temperature (up to 3000 K): CAD design of components for ultra-high-vacuum environments, 3D-printed mounts and adapters, integration of data acquisition systems and experiment automation in Python.

As an undergraduate researcher supervised by Prof. Leandro Mercês, he developed adaptive devices and origami actuators based on functional materials, using multi-material prototyping (TPU + PLA), 4D-printing concepts and mechanical characterisation of deformable structures. His undergraduate thesis, carried out at the University of Campinas (Unicamp) under Prof. Carina Marconi Germer, investigates electroactive-polymer actuators and origami-inspired architectures for biomechanical prostheses. As a FAPESP fellow (PAPL programme, 2025) at the Federal University of Rio Grande do Norte, he applied FFT, Lomb–Scargle periodograms and wavelets to time-series analysis, with application to experimental modal analysis.

He works with parametric CAD (Fusion 360, SolidWorks, Rhinoceros + Grasshopper), design for additive manufacturing, printing of PLA, PETG, TPU and composites, scientific programming in Python and MATLAB, computer vision (OpenCV) and automated data acquisition. His research interests include electroactive actuators, adaptive structures, soft robotics, scientific instrumentation, additive manufacturing and experimental modal analysis.
